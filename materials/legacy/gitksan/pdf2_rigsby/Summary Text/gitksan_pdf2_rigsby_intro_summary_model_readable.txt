Gitksan grammar reference summary: PDF 2 (Rigsby grammar introduction excerpts)
===============================================================================

This summary is derived from the corresponding grammar PDF and is intended to match the table-style summary image for controlled text-vs-image experiments.

Document scope
--------------
Source: Excerpts from the introductory chapter of Bruce Rigsby's Gitksan Grammar.
Main argument: The text treats Gitksan as a language, not merely a dialect, and discusses language identity, variation, and community history.
Main value for translation: This PDF gives sociolinguistic and historical context more than detailed morphosyntax; it can help with dialect labels, proper names, and cautious interpretation.

Language status and variation
-----------------------------
Language vs dialect: Gitksan speakers insist on calling Gitksan a language; the author frames language/dialect as social and empirical, not only structural.
Competence by generation: At the time described, older people were generally fluent, many middle-aged speakers were English-dominant, and younger adults included many semispeakers.
Language shift: English had become the vernacular for most Gitksan people; no monolingual Gitksan speakers and no children growing up only in Gitksan are reported.
Grammar implication: A grammar must account for variability caused by intergenerational differences in competence and command.

Cultural and translation context
--------------------------------
Place names: The Gitksan homeland is described as a humanized landscape with many place names and associated legends/historical narratives.
Knowledge domains: The text mentions folk knowledge of animals and plants, origins and uses, and high culture of chiefs and Indian doctors.
Translation caution: Unreflective translation into English can reduce and simplify Gitksan cultural knowledge and meanings.

Contact and related languages
-----------------------------
Coast/Nass contact: Gitksan people traveled to the coast and lower Nass River for oolachen/candlefish, trade, ceremonies, and intermarriage.
Coast Tsimshian: Knowing Coast Tsimshian was prestigious; chiefs used it in public speeches and ceremonies, and many songs were in it.
Nisga'a relation: The text discusses the Gitksan/Nisga'a relationship and their connections with Coast Tsimshian.
Historical comparison: The excerpt references Tsimshianic, Nass-Gitksan, Southern Tsimshian, Penutian debates, and numeral diffusion.

Practical cues for experiments
------------------------------
Use carefully: This PDF is weaker than PDF 1 for direct sentence-level translation grammar, because most pages are introduction, history, and bibliography.
Useful signals: Dialect identity, contact languages, named groups/places, and language shift context may still affect interpretation of examples.
OCR caution: The scanned PDF has noisy text extraction, so image input may preserve layout and spelling better than raw OCR.
