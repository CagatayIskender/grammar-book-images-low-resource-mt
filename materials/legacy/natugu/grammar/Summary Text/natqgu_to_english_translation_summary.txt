Natqgu to English Translation Summary
=====================================


1. Big picture
--------------


Natqgu is an Oceanic language with:

- Verb-initial transitive clauses: V A O
- Intransitive clauses usually: S V
- Many grammatical meanings attached to the verb as prefixes, suffixes, and enclitics
- A minimal vs. augmented person-number system
- Many important distinctions in demonstratives, possession, applicatives, directionals, and negation

The most important translation rule is:

Do not translate word by word from the root only. Translate the whole verb complex and check all enclitics.


2. Orthography and vowel symbols
--------------------------------


Natqgu uses some letters as vowel symbols.

Natqgu letter      | Approximate sound | Translation note                          
-------------------+-------------------+-------------------------------------------
a                  | /a/               | normal vowel                              
e                  | /e/               | normal vowel                              
i                  | /i/               | normal vowel                              
o                  | /o/               | normal vowel                              
u                  | /u/               | normal vowel                              
c                  | /ɔ/               | older spelling â                          
q                  | /ü/               | high front rounded vowel, older spelling ü
r                  | /ɵ/               | central rounded vowel, older spelling ö   
x                  | /æ/               | older spelling ä                          
z                  | /ə/               | schwa, older spelling ë                   
vowel + apostrophe | nasal vowel       | example: a’, e’                           


Important:

c, q, r, x, z may be vowels in Natqgu. Do not treat them automatically as consonants.


3. Basic clause order
---------------------


Clause type                             | Natqgu order | English order
----------------------------------------+--------------+--------------
Intransitive with noun subject          | S V          | S V          
Intransitive with only subject enclitic | V S possible | S V          
Transitive                              | V A O        | A V O        


Example interpretation:

Natqgu: V A O  
means  
English: A V O

So when translating a transitive clause, the first verb may be followed by the agent, then the object.


4. Pronouns and person-number system
------------------------------------


Natqgu does not simply use singular and plural. It uses minimal and augmented.

Free pronouns


Person-number | Natqgu | English      
--------------+--------+--------------
1 minimal     | ni=nge | I / me       
1+2 minimal   | ni=gi  | you and I    
2 minimal     | ni=mq  | you singular 
3 minimal     | ni=de  | he / she / it
1 augmented   | ni=gr  | we           
1+2 augmented | ni=gu  | we inclusive 
2 augmented   | ni=mu  | you plural   
3 augmented   | ni=dr  | they         


Subject enclitic cues


Meaning       | Common subject marker              
--------------+------------------------------------
I             | =x                                 
you singular  | =u                                 
he / she / it | =le or zero                        
we            | =kr                                
you plural    | =amu                               
they          | often nz- / tz- / na- plus enclitic


Important:

Subject and object information may be attached to the verb, not written as a separate word.


5. Demonstratives
-----------------


Natqgu demonstratives encode both distance and whether the anchor is speaker or listener.

Form | Meaning | Anchor           
-----+---------+------------------
la   | near    | listener-anchored
lc   | far     | listener-anchored
ka   | near    | speaker-anchored 
kc   | far     | speaker-anchored 


Important:

kc is very frequent and can also function like “that,” “which,” or sometimes “if.”

Plural may appear on demonstratives:

Pattern                | Meaning               
-----------------------+-----------------------
demonstrative + -ng(q) | plural “those / these”


Translation cue:

Plural may be marked on the demonstrative instead of the noun.

7. Nouns and possession
-----------------------


Natqgu has direct and indirect possession.

Direct possession


Direct possession is common with:

- Body parts
- Many kinship terms
- Inalienable relationships

Pattern:

possessed noun + possessor

Example structure:

head man = “man’s head”

With pronouns, the pronoun marker can attach directly:

head=his = “his head”

Indirect possession


Indirect possession uses a genitive or possessive classifier.

Pattern:

noun + genitive/classifier + possessor

Translation cue:

If a genitive or classifier appears between two nouns, translate it as possession, belonging, association, use, food/drink relation, or responsibility depending on context.


8. Nominalization cues
----------------------


Natqgu often turns verbs into noun-like expressions.

Pattern            | Function                   | English cue          
-------------------+----------------------------+----------------------
kx + verb          | participant nominalization | one who does X       
nz- + verb + -ngr  | action nominalization      | doing X              
z- + stative verb  | property noun              | X-ness, e.g. goodness
lr- + place/people | toponymic noun             | person/people from X 


Important:

kx can mean more than “that.” It may create “one who...” or “that which...” expressions.


9. Verb complex overview
------------------------


Natqgu verbs can contain many parts.

Basic map:

pre-core + verb root/stem + core applicative + post-core modifiers + peripheral applicative + directionals + enclitics

Translation rule:

Translate the whole verb chain, not just the root.

A verb may include:

- Subject
- Object
- Direction
- Aspect
- Negation
- Applicative meaning
- Intensity
- Repetition
- Reflexive or reciprocal meaning

11. Directionals
----------------


Natqgu uses directionals very often.

Geometric directionals


Form | Meaning
-----+--------
-lz  | up     
-tr  | in     
-px  | out    
-o   | down   


Personal directionals


Form | Meaning                            
-----+------------------------------------
-mq  | hither, toward the discourse center
-bz  | yon, away from the discourse center


Important:

A verb can have one geometric directional and one personal directional.

Translation cue:

Directionals can add English words like “up,” “down,” “in,” “out,” “come,” “go,” “toward,” or “away.”


12. Applicative -ngr
--------------------


-ngr is a peripheral applicative.

It often adds or licenses an extra participant.

Possible English cues:

- about
- for
- to
- toward
- with respect to
- affecting someone/something

Important:

After -ngr, a third person minimal subject may appear as Set II =de when no personal directional comes between -ngr and the subject marker.

Translation cue:

When you see -ngr, look for an extra argument or relationship that English may express with a preposition.


13. Passive
-----------


Main passive pattern:

nz- + verb + zero subject

Meaning:

be VERBed / get VERBed

Important:

The prefix nz- has multiple functions, so check context.

It can mark:

- third person augmented
- nominalization
- passive

Passive cue:

If nz- appears without a third person augmented subject, it may be passive.

Also:

na- and tz- can indicate passive when no third person augmented subject is present.


14. Negation
------------


Natqgu negation may be discontinuous.

Pattern     | English meaning
------------+----------------
tr- ... -u  | not            
tr- ... =ka | not yet        
trpnzngr    | none / not any 
trpengr     | not any more   
trgalr      | never          


Important:

The negative meaning may appear at both the beginning and end of the verb complex.

Translation cue:

Do not miss the final negative marker. A verb may look positive at first but become negative later.


15. High-impact translation rules
---------------------------------


1. Check whether the clause is transitive.  
   If yes, Natqgu may use V A O, but English needs A V O.

2. Read the full verb complex.  
   Prefixes, suffixes, and enclitics may change the meaning strongly.

3. Do not treat c q r x z as ordinary consonants.  
   They may be vowel letters.

4. Look for subject and object enclitics.  
   Arguments may be attached to the verb.

5. Watch for kc.  
   It may be a demonstrative, subordinator, or relativizer.

6. Watch for -ngr.  
   It often requires an English preposition or extra participant.

7. Watch for directionals.  
   They can encode motion, orientation, or discourse viewpoint.

8. Watch for discontinuous negation.  
   A negative prefix may be completed by a suffix or enclitic later.

9. Plural may be on demonstratives, not nouns.

10. Possession type matters.  
    Direct possession often means body parts or kinship. Indirect possession uses classifiers or genitives.
