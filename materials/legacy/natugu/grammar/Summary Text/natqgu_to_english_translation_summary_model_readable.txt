Natqgu to English Translation Guide
===================================

Use this guide as grammar support when translating Natqgu sentences into English.

The most important translation rule:
Do not translate word by word from the root only. Translate the whole verb complex and check all enclitics.

1. Big Picture
--------------

Natqgu is an Oceanic language with:
- Verb-initial transitive clauses: V A O.
- Intransitive clauses usually: S V.
- Many grammatical meanings attached to the verb as prefixes, suffixes, and enclitics.
- A minimal vs. augmented person-number system.
- Important distinctions in demonstratives, possession, applicatives, directionals, and negation.

2. Orthography and Vowel Symbols
--------------------------------

Natqgu uses some letters as vowel symbols. Do not treat c, q, r, x, and z automatically as consonants.

Vowel and spelling cues:
- a = /a/
- e = /e/
- i = /i/
- o = /o/
- u = /u/
- c = /ɔ/, older spelling â
- q = high front rounded vowel /ü/, older spelling ü
- r = central rounded vowel /ɵ/, older spelling ö
- x = /æ/, older spelling ä
- z = schwa /ə/, older spelling ë
- vowel + apostrophe = nasal vowel, for example a' or e'

Translation cue:
c, q, r, x, and z may be vowels inside words. This matters when recognizing roots and affixes.

3. Basic Clause Order
---------------------

Intransitive clause with noun subject:
- Natqgu order: S V
- English order: S V

Intransitive clause with only subject enclitic:
- Natqgu order: V S possible.
- English order: S V.

Transitive clause:
- Natqgu order: V A O
- English order: A V O

Example:
If Natqgu has Verb + Agent + Object, translate it as Agent + Verb + Object in English.

4. Pronouns and Person-Number
-----------------------------

Natqgu does not simply use singular and plural. It uses minimal and augmented.

Free pronouns:
- ni=nge = I / me
- ni=gi = you and I
- ni=mq = you singular
- ni=de = he / she / it
- ni=gr = we
- ni=gu = we inclusive
- ni=mu = you plural
- ni=dr = they

Common subject enclitic cues:
- =x = I
- =u = you singular
- =le or zero = he / she / it
- =kr = we
- =amu = you plural
- nz-, tz-, or na- plus enclitic often indicates they.

Translation cue:
Subject and object information may be attached to the verb instead of appearing as separate words.

5. Demonstratives
-----------------

Natqgu demonstratives encode distance and whether the anchor is the speaker or listener.

Common forms:
- la = near, listener-anchored
- lc = far, listener-anchored
- ka = near, speaker-anchored
- kc = far, speaker-anchored

Important:
kc is very frequent and can also function like "that," "which," or sometimes "if."

Plural demonstratives:
- demonstrative + -ng or -ngq can mean "these" or "those."

Translation cue:
Plural may be marked on the demonstrative instead of on the noun.

6. Numerals
-----------

Common numerals:
- esz' = one
- li = two
- tq = three
- pwx = four
- nzlvqn = five
- esz'mz = six
- rlimz = seven
- rtqmz = eight
- rpwxmz = nine
- nzpnu = ten
- nzpnu-li = twenty
- trtqki = one hundred
- siu = one thousand

Ordinals:
- kai = first
- kra- + cardinal number = other ordinal numbers

7. Nouns and Possession
-----------------------

Natqgu has direct and indirect possession.

Direct possession:
- Common with body parts, kinship terms, and inalienable relationships.
- Pattern: possessed noun + possessor.
- Example structure: "head man" means "the man's head."
- With pronouns, the pronoun marker can attach directly, as in "head=his."

Indirect possession:
- Uses a genitive marker or possessive classifier.
- Pattern: noun + genitive/classifier + possessor.

Translation cue:
If a genitive or classifier appears between two nouns, translate it as possession, belonging,
association, use, food/drink relation, or responsibility depending on context.

8. Nominalization Cues
----------------------

Natqgu often turns verbs into noun-like expressions.

Common patterns:
- kx + verb = participant nominalization, "one who does X" or "that which does X."
- nz- + verb + -ngr = action nominalization, "doing X."
- z- + stative verb = property noun, such as "goodness."
- lr- + place/people = toponymic noun, person or people from a place.

Important:
kx can mean more than "that." It may create "one who..." or "that which..." expressions.

9. Verb Complex
---------------

Basic verb-complex map:
pre-core + verb root/stem + core applicative + post-core modifiers + peripheral applicative + directionals + enclitics

A verb complex may include:
- Subject
- Object
- Direction
- Aspect
- Negation
- Applicative meaning
- Intensity
- Repetition
- Reflexive or reciprocal meaning

Translation rule:
Translate the whole verb chain, not just the root.

10. Common Verb Pieces
----------------------

Common forms and meanings:
- ti = transitive marker
- -tx = intensifier
- -ne = distributive
- -lz = up
- -tr = in
- -px = out
- -o = down
- -etr = reciprocal or reflexive
- mou = again
- kz = also
- =pe = change of state
- =pnz = completive
- =ka = not yet
- =pwz = just, only, or exactly

11. Directionals
----------------

Geometric directionals:
- -lz = up
- -tr = in
- -px = out
- -o = down

Personal directionals:
- -mq = hither, toward the discourse center
- -bz = yon, away from the discourse center

Important:
A verb can have one geometric directional and one personal directional.

Translation cue:
Directionals can add English meanings like "up," "down," "in," "out," "come," "go,"
"toward," or "away."

12. Applicative -ngr
--------------------

-ngr is a peripheral applicative. It often adds or licenses an extra participant.

Possible English cues:
- about
- for
- to
- toward
- with respect to
- affecting someone or something

Important:
After -ngr, a third-person minimal subject may appear as Set II =de when no personal
directional comes between -ngr and the subject marker.

Translation cue:
When you see -ngr, look for an extra argument or relationship that English may express
with a preposition.

13. Passive
-----------

Main passive pattern:
- nz- + verb + zero subject = "be VERBed" or "get VERBed."

Important:
The prefix nz- has multiple functions. It can mark:
- third-person augmented
- nominalization
- passive

Passive cue:
If nz- appears without a third-person augmented subject, it may be passive.
na- and tz- can indicate passive when no third-person augmented subject is present.

14. Negation
------------

Natqgu negation may be discontinuous.

Common patterns:
- tr- ... -u = not
- tr- ... =ka = not yet
- trpnzngr = none / not any
- trpengr = not any more
- trgalr = never

Important:
The negative meaning may appear at both the beginning and end of the verb complex.

Translation cue:
Do not miss the final negative marker. A verb may look positive at first but become negative later.

15. High-Impact Translation Rules
---------------------------------

Use these as a final checklist:
1. If the clause is transitive, Natqgu may use V A O, but English needs A V O.
2. Read the full verb complex. Prefixes, suffixes, and enclitics can strongly change the meaning.
3. Do not treat c, q, r, x, and z as ordinary consonants; they may be vowel letters.
4. Look for subject and object enclitics. Arguments may be attached to the verb.
5. Watch for kc. It may be a demonstrative, subordinator, or relativizer.
6. Watch for -ngr. It often requires an English preposition or extra participant.
7. Watch for directionals. They can encode motion, orientation, or discourse viewpoint.
8. Watch for discontinuous negation. A negative prefix may be completed by a suffix or enclitic later.
9. Plural may be marked on demonstratives, not nouns.
10. Possession type matters. Direct possession often involves body parts or kinship. Indirect possession uses classifiers or genitives.
