LEZGI GRAMMAR SUMMARY FOR TRANSLATION

Source: Haspelmath, A Grammar of Lezgian, excerpt in Lezgi_Grammar_PDF.pdf.
Use this as supporting grammar context for Lezgi-to-English translation.

High-level profile
- Lezgi morphology is strongly suffixing and agglutinating.
- Nouns, adjectives, and verbs are morphologically distinct.
- Clauses are generally head-final. SOV is preferred, although other orders occur, especially in speech.
- Noun phrases are head-final: genitive-noun, adjective-noun, numeral-noun, demonstrative-noun.
- Postpositions follow their complements.
- Lezgi shows ergative alignment in ordinary transitive clauses.
- There is no person-number agreement in noun phrases or on finite verbs.
- Full noun phrase arguments can be omitted when recoverable from context; personal pronouns are used when needed.

Nominal morphology
- Nouns inflect for number: singular and plural.
- Nouns inflect for case: Absolutive, Ergative, Genitive, Dative, Essive, Elative, Directive.
- Localizations combine with locative cases: Ad, Sub, Post, Super, In.
- The combinations create forms such as Adessive, Adelative, Addirective, Subessive, Subelative, Subdirective, Postessive, Postelative, Postdirective, Superessive, Superelative, Superdirective, Inessive, and Inelative.
- All cases except the Absolutive are based on a special oblique stem. The oblique stem suffix can be idiosyncratic for many nouns.
- Plural is commonly marked before case morphology.

Adjectives
- Adjectives can take a substantivizing suffix -di, as in "new one".
- Adjectives can form adverbs with suffixes such as -(di)z or -dakaz.
- Adjectives can appear in predicative use.

Verb morphology
- Verbs inflect for tense-aspect, negation, several moods, and non-finite forms.
- Finite verbs do not agree with person or number.
- The grammar gives gun "give" as the model verb.

Derivation
- Important nominal derivation: abstract suffix -wal, as in "new-ness".
- Causative verb derivation uses -(a)r and can turn an intransitive verb into a transitive verb.
- Borrowed or productive derivational material includes nominal -ci, adjectival -lu and -suz, and verbal -lamisun.

Clause alignment and argument marking
- Lezgi has ergative case-marking patterns.
- Intransitive subject: Absolutive.
- Transitive agent: Ergative.
- Transitive patient/theme: Absolutive.
- Dative subjects occur with some experiential verbs, including verbs like "see".
- Dative also marks goals and recipients in many examples.

Examples of core patterns
- "The brother came back home": brother is ABS, house is DAT, verb is AOR.
- "The sister sent the brother home": sister is ERG, brother is ABS, house is DAT, verb is AOR.
- "The sister saw the brother": sister is DAT, brother is ABS, verb is AOR.

Subordination and clause combining
- Subordinate clauses are usually non-finite.
- Subordinate clauses generally precede the main clause.
- Relative clauses use participles.
- Participles do not have a fixed orientation, so they can relativize many different constituents.
- Complement clauses have several major strategies:
  - Masdar complements.
  - Infinitival complements.
  - Participial complements.
- Clause chaining often uses converbs rather than coordination with "and".
- Aorist converbs can express actions like "having turned around".
- Specialized converbs form adverbial clauses, including posterior and temporal relations.

Questions
- Polar questions are marked by the interrogative verb suffix -ni.
- Content questions normally keep the interrogative pronoun in situ.
- Content questions do not use the interrogative verb suffix in the same way as polar questions.

Comparison
- Inequality comparison marks the standard of comparison in the Superelative case.
- The adjective itself is not specially marked for comparison.

Glossing and notation conventions
- Zero categories are usually not shown in morphemic glosses.
- A period in a noun form separates the lexical stem from a semantically empty oblique-stem suffix, as in mother.OBL-DAT style forms.
- Ergative is formally zero on the oblique stem, but it is shown in glosses because it contrasts with Absolutive.
- Personal pronoun Ergative and Absolutive forms are treated as unanalyzable.
- Subordinate clause boundaries are shown in brackets in glossed examples.
- If Lezgi text contains a hyphen, the morpheme gloss may use an equals sign to avoid confusing lexical hyphens with morpheme boundaries.

Phonology and orthography cues
- Lezgi has vowel syncope, especially pretonic high-vowel syncope after voiceless obstruents.
- Syncope is not consistently reflected in the orthography.
- Lost high vowels can leave palatalization or labialization on a preceding consonant.
- Vowel harmony is described for palatal and labial features in the first two syllables.
- Some consonant alternations affect noun forms, especially between Absolutive Singular and other forms.
- Stress is generally on the second syllable of the root; some Arabic loans may show third-syllable stress.

Translation reminders for the model
- Use case marking more than word order when deciding grammatical roles.
- Treat ERG as a strong cue for the transitive agent.
- Treat ABS as the intransitive subject or transitive patient/theme.
- Treat DAT as goal, recipient, or experiencer depending on the verb.
- Expect omitted arguments when the context makes them recoverable.
- Expect subordinate clauses before main clauses.
- In final translation, produce natural English rather than a word-by-word rendering.
